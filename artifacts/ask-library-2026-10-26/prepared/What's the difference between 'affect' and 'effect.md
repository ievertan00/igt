**Q:** What's the difference between 'affect' and 'effect'?

The easiest way to remember the difference is that **affect** is usually a verb (an action), and **effect** is usually a noun (a result).

## The Pattern
*   **Affect (Verb):** To influence or produce a change in something.
*   **Effect (Noun):** The result or consequence of an action.

## Examples
*   **Affect (Action)**
    *   The heavy rain will **affect** the traffic flow.
    *   How does the new policy **affect** your daily routine?

*   **Effect (Result)**
    *   The medicine had a positive **effect** on her health.
    *   The special **effects** in the movie were stunning.

## Caveats
While the rule above works 95% of the time, there are rare exceptions where the words swap roles:

1.  **Effect as a verb:** This means "to bring about" or "to cause to happen." It is mostly used in formal or administrative contexts (e.g., "to effect change").
2.  **Affect as a noun:** This is used almost exclusively in psychology to describe a person's outward expression of emotion (e.g., "The patient displayed a flat affect").

## Rule of Thumb
If you can replace the word with "influence," use **affect**.
If you can replace the word with "result," use **effect**.
