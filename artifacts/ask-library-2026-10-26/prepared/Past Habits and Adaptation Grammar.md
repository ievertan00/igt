# Past Habits and Adaptation Grammar

**Date**: 2026-05-28 17:34

This consultation covered three distinct grammatical concepts: the difference between `used to`, `be used to`, and `get used to`; and the distinction between `used to` and `would` when discussing the past.

## Past Habits: Used To vs. Would

When discussing past routines, `used to` and `would` overlap but are not interchangeable. The key difference is that `used to` can describe both **past habits** and **past states** (conditions or facts that were true but are no longer true, e.g., *I used to live in Chicago*). Conversely, `would` can *only* be used for **repeated, habitual actions** and cannot describe a state (e.g., *I would live in Chicago* is incorrect).

## Adaptation and Familiarity: Used To vs. Be/Get Used To

These three phrases describe different aspects of familiarity:

*   **`Used to` (Past Fact):** Describes a past reality or habit that is no longer true. (Structure: `Subject + used to + base verb`). *Example: I used to smoke.* 
*   **`Be used to` (Current State):** Means something is normal or familiar *now*. It describes an existing state of comfort. (Structure: `Subject + be + used to + gerund/noun`). *Example: I am used to the cold weather.* 
*   **`Get used to` (Process):** Describes the *process* of becoming accustomed to something. (Structure: `Subject + get + used to + gerund/noun`). *Example: It took me a month to get used to the noise.* 

**Crucial Rule:** Both `be used to` and `get used to` require a gerund (-ing) or a noun following them because they describe the *thing* being adapted to, not a simple past action.
