## Distinguishing Conjunctions from Prepositions
The primary difference between a conjunction and a preposition lies in the grammatical structure that follows the word.

*   **Conjunctions** connect two parts of a sentence (phrases or clauses). They are followed by a **subject + verb** (a clause).
*   **Prepositions** show the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another part of the sentence. They are followed by a **noun or pronoun** (the object of the preposition).

**Example Comparison:**
*   **Conjunction:** I stayed inside **because** it was raining. (*Because* + subject "it" + verb "was")
*   **Preposition:** I stayed inside **because of** the rain. (*Because of* + noun "the rain")

Note that some words, such as "after" or "since," can function as either a conjunction or a preposition depending on whether they are followed by a clause or a simple noun.

## Types of Conjunctions
Conjunctions are the "glue" of English and are categorized into three main types based on their function:

### 1. Coordinating Conjunctions
These connect words, phrases, or clauses of equal grammatical importance. A common mnemonic for these is **FANBOYS**:
*   **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o

### 2. Subordinating Conjunctions
These connect a dependent (subordinate) clause to an independent clause, establishing relationships of time, cause, condition, or concession. Common examples include:
*   **Cause/Reason:** *Since, As, In order that, So that*
*   **Contrast/Concession:** *Although, Even though, Though, While, Whereas, Even if*
*   **Time:** *When, Whenever, While, Before, After, Until, As soon as, Once*
*   **Condition:** *If, Unless, Provided that, As long as, In case*
*   **Comparison/Relation:** *As if, As though, Rather than, Whether*

### 3. Correlative Conjunctions
These are pairs of words that work together to join elements of equal importance:
*   *Either... or*
*   *Neither... nor*
*   *Not only... but also*
*   *Whether... or*
