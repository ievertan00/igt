Choosing the correct preposition depends on whether you are expressing direction/inclusion, purpose, or possession/composition.

## To vs. For
While both can sometimes be used interchangeably, they convey different nuances:

*   **To:** Indicates **direction, inclusion, or connection**. In technical contexts (e.g., "add a feature to the project"), *to* is preferred because it implies integrating a new element into an existing system or codebase.
*   **For:** Emphasizes **purpose or intended recipient**. Use *for* when you want to highlight why something is being done or who it benefits (e.g., "a feature for the users").

## To vs. Of
These prepositions are rarely interchangeable because they serve different grammatical functions:

*   **To:** Indicates movement toward a destination, a recipient of an action, or a means to an end (e.g., "a path to success" or "give it to him").
*   **Of:** Indicates **possession, composition, or specific characteristics**. It describes what something is made of or what it belongs to (e.g., "a cup of tea" or "the capital of France").
