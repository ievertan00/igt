Distinguishing between countable and uncountable nouns depends on whether a noun refers to a **discrete unit** or an **abstract/mass concept**. 

## The Core Difference
*   **Countable nouns** are individual things you can count with numbers (e.g., chair, fact). They have both singular and plural forms.
*   **Uncountable nouns** (mass nouns) represent substances, collections, or abstract ideas (e.g., water, information). They generally do not have a plural form.

## How to Detect Them
You can test a noun using these three rules:

1.  **The Number Test**: Can you put a number directly in front of it? 
    *   ✓ One chair (Countable) | ✗ One water (Uncountable)
2.  **The Article Test**: Does it make sense with "a" or "an"?
    *   ✓ A table (Countable) | ✗ A music (Uncountable)
3.  **The "Amount vs. Number" Rule**: 
    *   Use **many/few** for countable nouns.
    *   Use **much/little** or **amount of** for uncountable nouns.

## Common Categories of Uncountable Nouns
If you are unsure, check if the word falls into these common categories:
*   **Liquids & Substances**: water, coffee, gold, wood.
*   **Abstract Ideas**: advice, information, luck, time.
*   **Mass Nouns (Collections)**: furniture, luggage, money, evidence.
*   **Activities**: homework, research, shopping.

## Common Confusions: Category vs. Unit
English often features pairs where one word is uncountable (the general category) and the other is countable (the specific unit). 

| Uncountable (Category) | Countable (Unit) | Rule of Thumb |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Information** | **Fact** | Use 'information' for knowledge; 'fact' for a specific truth. |
| **Advice** | **Suggestion** | 'Advice' is the act of guidance; 'suggestion' is a specific idea. |
| **Furniture** | **Chair/Table** | 'Furniture' is the category; 'chair' is the individual item. |
| **Equipment** | **Tool/Machine** | 'Equipment' is general gear; 'tool' is a specific instrument. |
| **Work** | **Job** | 'Work' is the activity of labor; 'job' is a specific role or task. |

## The "Unit" Exception
You can count an uncountable substance by using a **container or unit of measurement**. This transforms the mass into a countable instance:
*   ✓ Two **bottles** of water.
*   ✓ A **piece** of advice.
*   ✓ An **item** of equipment.
*   ✓ A **bit** of work.
