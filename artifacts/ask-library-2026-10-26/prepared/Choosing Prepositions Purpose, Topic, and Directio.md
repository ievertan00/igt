# Choosing Prepositions: Purpose, Topic, and Direction

**Date**: 2026-05-29 09:48

## Prepositional Collocations and Usage

Correct preposition usage often depends on **collocation**—the natural pairing of words. For instance, when describing a document, the choice between `for` and `on` signals different relationships:

*   Use **`for`** when indicating the *purpose* or *intended recipient* (e.g., a handbook **for** beginners).
*   Use **`on`** when indicating the *subject matter* or *topic* (e.g., a handbook **on** grammar).

## Differentiating Core Prepositions

The prepositions `of`, `for`, `on`, `to`, and `by` signal distinct relationships:

*   **`OF` (Belonging/Composition):** Shows possession, origin, or what something is made of (e.g., the color **of** the sky).
*   **`FOR` (Purpose/Recipient/Duration):** Indicates the *reason* something is done, the intended recipient, or a length of time (e.g., a gift **for** my mother; studying **for** the exam).
*   **`ON` (Topic/Surface/Day):** Relates to a specific topic, a physical surface, or a specific day (e.g., a lecture **on** astrophysics; the book is **on** the table).
*   **`TO` (Direction/Destination):** Points to an endpoint or destination (e.g., driving **to** the coast).
*   **`BY` (Deadline/Means):** Often relates to a deadline or the agent performing an action (e.g., finished **by** Friday).

## Purpose vs. Direction (`To` vs. `For`)

When distinguishing between `to` and `for` regarding goals or movement:

*   Use **`TO`** when describing a physical **destination** or endpoint (e.g., walking **to** the library).
*   Use **`FOR`** when describing the **reason** or *purpose* behind an action (e.g., studying **for** the test).

**Rule of Thumb:** If you can replace the preposition with 'toward' or 'at the location of', use `to`. If you can replace it with 'because of' or 'with the goal of', use `for`.
