# Certainty Modifiers and Adverb Placement

**Date**: 2026-05-29 11:26

## The Certainty Scale
These modifiers occupy distinct points on a spectrum of probability: `unlikely` (low chance) → `likely` (good chance) → `probably` (high chance) → `definitely` (certain) → `bound to` (inevitable/circumstantially guaranteed).

## Grammatical Patterns & Usage
- **`likely` / `unlikely`**: Function primarily as adjectives. They pair with `be` + `to` or introduce a `that` clause (e.g., `It is likely to rain.` / `She’s unlikely to agree.`). In informal American English, `likely` can act as an adverb (`He’ll likely agree`), but standard/formal English prefers `probably` or `He’s very likely to`.
- **`probably` / `definitely`**: Are adverbs of certainty. They modify the verb directly and never take `to` (✗ `probably to happen`). They express subjective prediction (`probably` hedges; `definitely` emphasizes strong personal certainty).
- **`be bound to`**: A fixed phrase signaling objective inevitability or logical consequence. Always requires the `be` verb (e.g., `If you skip practice, you’re bound to lose your edge.`).

## Placement Rules for Adverbs of Certainty
`probably` and `definitely` slot into the **middle of the verb phrase**, anchored to the first verb:
- **After `be` or auxiliary/modal verbs** (`am/is/are`, `will/can/should`, `have/has`, `do/does`): `She is probably tired.` / `They can definitely help.` / `I have probably eaten.`
- **Before the main verb** (when no auxiliary is present): `He probably forgot.` / `I definitely agree.`
- **With `be going to` / continuous forms**: Anchor to the initial `be` verb: `It’s probably going to rain.`
- **In negative sentences**: `probably` typically precedes the negative word to soften the statement (`He probably won’t show up.`), while `definitely` follows it for strong emphasis (`I definitely don’t want to go.`).

## Rule of Thumb
Find the **first verb** in your verb phrase. Place `probably` or `definitely` immediately **after** it. If there’s only one verb, place the adverb **before** it. Use `likely/unlikely` for objective probability assessments, `probably/definitely` for everyday predictions and emphasis, and `be bound to` for unavoidable, circumstance-driven outcomes.
