The distinction between restrictive and non-restrictive clauses depends on whether the information provided is essential to identifying the noun.

## Restrictive vs. Non-Restrictive Clauses

**Restrictive Clauses** provide essential information. Without them, the identity of the noun remains unclear or the sentence loses its primary meaning.
*   **Punctuation:** No commas are used.
*   **Pronouns:** You can use *that*, *who*, or *which*.
*   **Example:** The book *that I borrowed from the library* is overdue. (The clause identifies which specific book is being discussed.)

**Non-Restrictive Clauses** provide extra, non-essential information (a "side note"). The identity of the noun remains clear even if the clause is removed.
*   **Punctuation:** Must be set off by commas.
*   **Pronouns:** You must use *who*, *whom*, or *which*; **never** use *that*.
*   **Example:** My sister, *who lives in New York*, is coming to visit. (The identity of the person—my sister—is already clear.)

## Rules for Omitting Relative Pronouns
You can omit a relative pronoun (*that*, *who*, or *which*) only when it functions as the **object** of the relative clause and is not preceded by a preposition.

### When you CAN omit them:
This occurs in restrictive clauses where the pronoun is followed immediately by a new subject (noun or pronoun).
*   The book [that] I bought is great. $ightarrow$ *The book I bought is great.*
*   The man [whom] I met was kind. $ightarrow$ *The man I met was kind.*

### When you CANNOT omit them:
1.  **When it is the Subject:** If the pronoun is the subject of its own clause, it must remain.
    *   *Correct:* The man **who** saw me was tall.
2.  **In Non-Restrictive Clauses:** Because these are set off by commas, the structure requires the pronoun to remain for clarity.
    *   *Correct:* My brother, **whom** I met yesterday, is a doctor.
3.  **When Preceded by a Preposition:** If a preposition (e.g., *in*, *to*, *with*) directly precedes the pronoun, it must stay.\n    *   *Correct:* The house in **which** I live is small.
    *   *Alternative:* The house [that] I live in is small.

## Summary Rule of Thumb
To decide if you can omit a pronoun, ask: **Is there a new subject immediately following the pronoun?** If yes, and it is a restrictive clause (no commas), you may drop it. Otherwise, keep it.
